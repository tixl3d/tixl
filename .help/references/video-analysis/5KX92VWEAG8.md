A beginners meet-up that first explains TiXL's real-time CPU/GPU model and the three performance bottlenecks (poly count, fill-rate/overdraw, memory), then builds a "Blender donut" from scratch — a ray-marched torus with materials, a faked floor shadow, point lights, an image-based environment, color grading, and scattered sprinkles — closing with a workflow method for reproducing a reference image.

## Mentions
- 12:36→18:45 [ui:Graph] · explained · scripted · Concept · 78% — The three operator output colours encode where data lives: a texture lives on the GPU and is regenerated each time you modify it, a buffer is an opaque GPU pointer you can't read back but can draw from, and a green node is a draw/command. Knowing the colour tells you what an output can and can't do.
- 14:00→15:35 [ui:EvaluationContext] · explained · scripted · Concept · 72% — Why TiXL splits work CPU-side (positions, sizes, names, file loading) from GPU-side (the buffers and textures it generates), and what that division costs you in flexibility.
- 21:10→23:45 [ui:PerformanceMonitor] · in-depth · scripted · Performance · 88% — Read the small numbers under the frame rate, not just the FPS: compute-shader count, triangle count, and draw calls reveal which of the three bottlenecks you've hit. Jittery (not steady-slow) frame times on a laptop mean thermal throttling, not your scene.
- 21:50→24:40 [GridPoints] · explained · experiment · Performance · 80% — Each drawn point becomes two triangles (a quad), so a count in the millions tanks the frame rate; pushing the count up via the infinity slider is the quickest way to feel the poly-count ceiling on your hardware.
- 24:40→26:00 [DrawPoints] · explained · experiment · Performance · 80% — Overdraw demo: with few points but a large point radius, the same pixels get shaded repeatedly and the GPU stalls — shrink the radius and performance snaps back. Watch fill-rate, not just object count.
- 27:00→32:45 [RenderTarget] · in-depth · scripted · Concept · 82% — Feed it a camera and the magic 0,0 resolution to capture a scene into a texture, then crank the texture to 8K to watch pixel count (and effect cost) explode — this is where a heavy post chain actually gets paid for, in millions of pixels per frame.
- 29:42→33:00 [ColorGrade] · explained · scripted · Concept · 80% — Image effects don't overwrite their input; TiXL allocates a fresh same-size buffer for the result so the untouched original is still available downstream (e.g. to recombine with a glow pass).
- 30:20→32:20 [Bloom] · explained · scripted · Performance · 78% — An expensive effect because it runs multiple blur cascades; at 8K those passes are what drops you off 60 fps, so it's a prime candidate to apply at a reduced resolution.
- 38:40→42:00 [ui:SearchWindow] · explained · scripted · Tip · 82% — Long-press the background and type to search; synonyms and fuzzy/substring matching mean "donut" finds the [TorusMesh] and even partial initials like "tm" rank [TorusMesh] and [TransformMesh] first — so small typos still land the op.
- 39:30→43:00 [TorusMesh] · explained · scripted · Example · 80% — Searching "donut" yields it (a torus is the graphics term); hover its output to confirm it's a mesh buffer, then click the output to get usage-ranked suggestions for what to wire next.
- 41:15→42:40 [DrawMesh] · explained · scripted · Example · 78% — The statistically most common follow-up when you click a mesh-buffer output; TiXL ranks connection suggestions by how people actually wire that output type.
- 42:00→43:00 [RaymarchField] · explained · scripted · Comparison · 75% — Swap an SDF's default shader-graph rendering for this to gain ambient-occlusion shading; its AO-distance knob adds contact shadow the cheaper path lacks. Pick it when you want the SDF surface to read as solid.
- 43:30→52:55 [ui:OutputWindow] · explained · scripted · Concept · 76% — In fill mode the viewer matches its window's vertical extent and pads left/right to the set aspect ratio, so dragging the panel reflows the view without distorting it.
- 44:40→47:00 [Camera] · explained · scripted · Gotcha · 82% — Without a camera operator the viewer's own orbit isn't stored anywhere; add one and pin its output so dragging the view writes into saveable position parameters instead of a throwaway preview.
- 48:30→53:50 [ui:Graph] · explained · scripted · Tip · 80% — The magnetic graph: long-press selects a single op, long-press-and-drag plus a shake disconnects it, and dropping a node near a connection snaps it into a vertical stacked batch — clicking a stacked node then drags the whole stack.
- 50:00→52:50 [NoiseDisplaceSDF] · explained · experiment · Example · 76% — Insert it on an SDF connection to break up mathematically-perfect geometry; dial the noise amount low so the shape keeps structure rather than dissolving.
- 53:00→55:00 [SetMaterial] · explained · scripted · Parameters · 82% — Works on both meshes and SDFs; lowering glossiness moves a surface from shiny toward a frosted matte look, and bypassing it shows the bare default shading underneath.
- 54:50→59:55 [ui:OperatorSettings] · explained · scripted · Tip · 78% — Rename any operator by clicking its title field or selecting and pressing Return; a renamed op shows quote marks, and its underlying type is still readable in the top-left corner when selected.
- 56:00→59:55 [ui:Graph] · in-depth · scripted · Gotcha · 84% — A disconnected operator costs nothing — TiXL ignores anything not wired into the graph, so duplicating (Ctrl+D pastes at the mouse) a material as a safety copy has zero performance impact until you drag it into the stack.
- 57:00→1:00:00 [SetMaterial] · in-depth · scripted · Gotcha · 84% — In a stack the last material wins: evaluation is pull-based from the output backwards, so a material lower (later) in the stack overrides earlier ones — set a default then override one object.
- 1:01:20→1:03:20 [ui:Graph] · explained · scripted · Concept · 80% — A multi-input (the angled socket) accepts any number of connections shown as blinking insertion points; wiring the same object twice into a group renders it twice, e.g. to build up opacity on a transparent object.
- 1:03:40→1:05:10 [QuadMesh] · explained · experiment · Example · 74% — Used as a floor plane; rotate it -90° on X to lay it flat (it's drawn edge-on otherwise) and scale to taste under the main object.
- 1:04:30→1:06:20 [DrawMesh] · explained · scripted · Concept · 82% — Backface culling explained: by default only front faces draw (as little as necessary for real-time), so a flat plane vanishes from behind — switch the cull side or disable culling to make it double-sided.
- 1:06:20→1:11:20 [ui:Gizmo] · explained · scripted · Tip · 76% — TiXL is a right-handed Y-up coordinate system (X right, Y up, Z toward camera) — unlike Blender's Z-up; rotations go counter-clockwise. The transform gizmo's coloured axes map directly to X/Y/Z.
- 1:09:40→1:11:20 [TransformMesh] · explained · scripted · Comparison · 76% — Choose it to move the geometry itself versus a downstream [Transform] that moves where the mesh is drawn; the distinction matters when later ops depend on point/object positions.
- 1:09:50→1:11:00 [ui:ParameterWindow] · passing · scripted · Tip · 70% — Right-click a value to copy and right-click-paste onto another op fills parameters by matching name, handy for transferring a translation between operators.
- 1:11:30→1:18:00 [PointLight] · explained · scripted · Gotcha · 80% — A default scene already has lighting on so beginners see something; create a point light for full control, but the still-lit far side comes from indirect/environment light, not this op. Order between point lights doesn't matter — last eight win.
- 1:13:50→1:17:00 [SetEnvironment] · explained · scripted · Concept · 80% — Supplies image-based / indirect lighting (HDRI-style); switch among built-in presets to relight the whole scene, set it to black to kill ambient, and toggle whether the environment itself is rendered as background.
- 1:16:00→1:18:00 [ui:Gizmo] · passing · scripted · Tip · 72% — Set gizmo visibility to "for selected only" so light and transform handles stop cluttering the view until you actually pick the operator.
- 1:18:20→1:23:00 [Blob] · explained · scripted · Example · 76% — A soft procedural shape used as a fake drop-shadow texture under an object; its preview thumbnail inherits the output aspect ratio, which is why you give it a fixed resolution so it stays round.
- 1:20:20→1:24:00 [ui:Graph] · in-depth · scripted · Gotcha · 82% — Z-fighting (flickering where two coplanar surfaces meet) and its fixes: nudge one slightly up, or disable the depth test — but with the test off the object draws regardless of depth, so you must reorder it in the group's multi-input to control what ends up on top.
- 1:21:20→1:24:00 [ui:EvaluationContext] · explained · scripted · Concept · 74% — What the Z-buffer is: a depth buffer parallel to the colour buffer that decides per-pixel whether something is drawn based on its distance from the camera plane.
- 1:23:25→1:24:30 [ui:ParameterWindow] · explained · scripted · Tip · 78% — On a layer/blend op, drag with the left mouse to set opacity and the right mouse to set luminosity directly on the parameter — a fast shortcut for tinting a fake shadow dark.
- 1:24:30→1:27:30 [ui:OutputSettings] · in-depth · scripted · Concept · 86% — The "magic" 0,0 resolution means "inherit the current output resolution"; set a real fixed resolution instead when a texture's aspect ratio must stay square regardless of the window, e.g. for a uniform shadow sprite.
- 1:28:40→1:30:30 [Blob] · explained · scripted · Tip · 74% — Add a negative-brightness blob over the image to make a vignette — darkening the corners with one shape, no dedicated effect needed.
- 1:29:30→1:34:00 [Glow] · explained · scripted · Parameters · 76% — Beyond intensity, its glow-gradient knob colour-tints the bloom by brightness, letting the bright core read one hue and the soft falloff another.
- 1:30:00→1:36:00 [ColorGrade] · in-depth · scripted · Parameters · 82% — Push highlights warm and shadows cool for the cinematic "pop" look; the gain/bias control is a contrast curve you drag left/right (steepen into an S for more contrast, flatten for less) and up/down to shift it, like a Photoshop curve in two parameters.
- 1:34:00→1:38:00 [ImageLevels] · explained · scripted · Tip · 65% — An "energy levels" analysis overlay that slices the image at a movable line and plots the colour there; a blinking warning marks where brightness clips and that highlight detail is lost for good.
- 1:37:50→1:40:00 [WaveForm] · in-depth · scripted · Concept · 80% — A per-column brightness scope (the film-industry "waveform") that reveals clipping and colour casts the eye misses — use it to spot where highlights blow out before grading, or to neutralise an existing cast channel by channel.
- 1:35:00→1:39:00 [ToneMapping] · explained · scripted · Comparison · 78% — The smartest way to tame over-bright pixels that would otherwise clip: prefer it over simply pulling gain or bloom intensity down, because it compresses highlights rather than crushing them.
- 1:41:20→1:48:00 [SetEnvironment] · in-depth · scripted · Gotcha · 84% — Its order relative to the camera matters: it renders the background first, before the camera is known, so placing it after the camera (and with an opaque, non-transparent source image) is required for a consistent background — the image-based-lighting math fails on a transparent input.
- 1:42:00→1:48:00 [RoundedRect] · passing · scripted · Example · 70% — Used as a quick environment/HDRI stand-in; give it fixed resolution so its aspect ratio doesn't follow the output window and warp the lighting.
- 1:43:00→1:46:00 [ui:ColorEditor] · explained · scripted · Tip · 76% — Click the brightness (B) field to toggle modes, and hold Ctrl while dragging to push a colour far past 1.0 into HDR — bright enough to light the scene more than a point light.
- 1:48:00→1:52:00 [SetFog] · explained · scripted · Parameters · 76% — Fades the scene toward a colour by camera distance; with too few floor subdivisions the fade reveals banding across large triangles, so subdivide heavy geometry to keep distance-based shading smooth.
- 1:48:30→1:50:00 [QuadMesh] · passing · scripted · Tip · 68% — On a large plane, add subdivisions even though they're invisible up close — sparse geometry can produce shading artifacts that extra triangles smooth out.
- 1:52:50→1:55:20 [GridPoints] · explained · scripted · Parameters · 80% — Cell mode keeps a fixed spacing between points as you add more; bounds mode instead fills a set volume by redistributing them — reach for cell when the gap matters, bounds when the extent matters. Drop the Y count to 1 for a flat layer.
- 1:55:30→1:58:30 [DrawMeshAtPoints] · explained · scripted · Example · 80% — Scatter a mesh (here a cylinder as a sprinkle) across a point set; it's the auto-suggested op when you connect points to a mesh, and it instances the geometry at every position.
- 1:58:30→2:03:00 [TransformPoints] · explained · experiment · Gotcha · 78% — Scale instances by transforming in point space, not object space — scaling in object space squashes the whole cloud toward the origin instead of resizing each instance in place.
- 2:01:40→2:03:20 [CylinderMesh] · explained · experiment · Performance · 74% — Prefer the simpler cylinder over a high-res beveled mesh; halving its column count is invisible until very low, so trimming subdivisions is free polygon savings.
- 2:03:00→2:06:00 [MoveToSDF] · in-depth · scripted · Example · 82% — Snap a scattered point cloud onto a surface by feeding it the points and an SDF field — the way to plant sprinkles on a torus; follow it with point-space transforms to align and randomise the now-surface-bound instances.
- 2:04:00→2:05:30 [RandomizePoints] · explained · experiment · Tip · 76% — Stack it as a processing step (vertical, Blender-modifier-stack style): scramble positions before snapping to a surface, then a second instance after to randomise rotation — order in the stack changes the result.
- 2:06:30→2:11:00 [PickInt] · passing · experiment · Tip · 62% — Drive a procedural choice from a counter through a pick op's multi-input to flip between variants (here swapping sprinkle materials) without rewiring.
- 2:07:40→2:09:00 [ui:Annotations] · passing · scripted · Tip · 72% — Press Shift+A to wrap selected operators in a named annotation group — useful for parking a finished "divergent" experiment before duplicating it and iterating on a cleaner version.
- 2:13:30→2:14:30 [SphereMesh] · passing · experiment · Example · 60% — Swapped in as an alternate sprinkle shape while iterating; any mesh can instance at the scatter points.
- 2:16:00→2:17:30 [RadialPoints] · passing · experiment · Example · 64% — Substituted for a grid of points to fan instances around a ring; widening its radius spreads the scattered shapes into new arrangements.
- 2:20:50→2:23:00 [CompareImages] · explained · scripted · Tip · 80% — Load a reference photo and put it side-by-side (or wipe) against your render to rebuild a look methodically — the core technique for learning by reproducing an image.
- 2:21:30→2:23:30 [LinearGradient] · explained · scripted · Example · 76% — Sample colours straight off a reference with Alt-click to recreate its background; flip the gradient direction if the picked endpoints come out reversed.
- 2:23:30→2:24:30 [NormalMap] · passing · scripted · Example · 66% — Feed noise through it into a material's normal input to fake surface bumps when matching a reference, then tune roughness to place the reflections.
