A live meet-up that rebuilds a viewer's water/particle scene from scratch, then dives deep into wrapping graphs into operators, time clips and time-remapping, the pull-based evaluation model, an SDF-driven fog scene with color grading, particle factor remapping, and a long open design discussion about multi-channel audio routing.

## Mentions
- 4:30→6:00 [LinePoints] [ParticleSystem] · explained · experiment · Example · 80% — Emitting from a line into a particle system is the minimal starting point for a falling-spray effect; pair it with a draw operator and an orbit camera before adding any forces.
- 5:55→7:10 [ParticleSystem] · explained · experiment · Parameters · 78% — Lifetime auto-computes from emit-rate vs. buffer size (100 particles/frame into a 100k buffer), so leave it at -1 until you slice the points, then set an explicit lifetime.
- 7:00→7:20 [DirectionalForce] · explained · experiment · Example · 82% — Drop one in as gravity to make emitted particles fall; stacking a second directional force lets you nudge flow direction, but a strong second force quickly dominates and looks fake.
- 7:30→8:10 [RandomizePoints] · explained · experiment · Example · 80% — Randomize position and per-axis rotation right after emission to break up uniform spray into a more organic scatter.
- 8:05→8:55 [SampleGradient] · explained · experiment · Tip · 72% — Remap particle lifetime through a white→blue gradient to color the spray by age without a separate color operator.
- 8:40→9:10 · explained · experiment · Tip · 70% — Randomize the F factor and feed it as a velocity multiplier so some particles fly fast and others slow, giving the stream natural speed variation.
- 9:20→10:30 [PlaneSDF] [FieldVolumeForce] · explained · experiment · Example · 82% — Build a collision floor by feeding a plane SDF into a field volume force; enabling collisions makes particles bounce off the field surface.
- 11:15→13:55 [CylinderSDF] [CombineSDF] · explained · experiment · Example · 80% — Subtract a tall, wide cylinder from a plane to carve a channel or basin into the collision field that particles then flow along.
- 13:20→14:00 [RotateAxis] · in-depth · experiment · Comparison · 80% — Rotating an SDF with a dedicated axis-rotate is cheaper than wrapping it in a full transform, which is slower for SDF stacks.
- 14:50→16:40 [NoiseDisplaceSDF] · explained · experiment · Example · 78% — Offset a flat collision field with noise displacement to roughen it; intersecting a smooth copy with a noisy copy keeps bumps only where they rise above the surface.
- 23:40→25:10 [VelocityForce] · in-depth · discussion · Concept · 72% — Pumps energy in or out along a particle's existing heading without changing direction, with min/max speed clamps so the flow can never exceed or drop below a set velocity.
- 26:30→29:30 [SetMaterial] · explained · experiment · Example · 75% — A collision force can stamp a material color on impact, so coloring the collider white and the base plane another color gives foam-on-contact tinting you can invert to taste.
- 30:25→35:10 [RaymarchField] · in-depth · discussion · Performance · 82% — The expensive part is ray-marching the distance field to find the surface (worst-case full step count per pixel); lowering max steps degrades the far field, and for particle collisions you skip surface rendering entirely so it stays cheap.
- 35:15→38:50 [Raster3dField] [PushPullSDF] · explained · experiment · Example · 70% — Raster3dField places a procedural texture onto SDF geometry; push-pull offsets the computed distance toward the surface like an extrude, and its amount input accepts a noise field to carve cobblestone-style relief.
- 36:40→37:10 [PerlinNoise] · passing · discussion · Concept · 60% — Named as the available 3D volume noise when a Voronoi/Worley pattern was wanted but isn't yet exposed as a field.
- 39:40→43:10 · in-depth · scripted · Tip · 85% — Wrap a sub-graph into a reusable operator by Ctrl-clicking a parameter to expose it, then "combine into symbol"; leaving a connected noodle inside auto-creates the matching output type.
- 43:10→45:10 · explained · scripted · Tip · 78% — Exposed parameters default to a slow virtual-slider scaling, but the "rotation" preset on a parameter sets min/max/speed and the degree unit so it behaves like every other rotation knob.
- 45:10→46:00 · explained · scripted · Tip · 72% — Saved parameter presets can be Alt-blended between, letting you crossfade whole looks on a complex operator.
- 45:50→47:30 [RenderTarget] [Layer2d] · explained · scripted · Example · 72% — Add a second texture output to a wrapped operator (right-click → add output, set type to texture), then a 2D layer lets you place that rendered image as a plane in 3D space.
- 53:50→57:10 [Switch] · explained · scripted · Concept · 80% — A switch's index selects which child branch is evaluated; -1 disables all, and unselected branches consume texture memory but no render cost, making it a cheap scene selector.
- 57:20→58:30 [Group] [Execute] · explained · scripted · Tip · 72% — Both expose an "is enabled" toggle, giving a second way to switch a scene branch on or off independent of any switch index.
- 58:30→1:01:50 [TimeClip] · in-depth · scripted · Concept · 85% — A time clip only gates whether its connected branch is visible during its span on the timeline; vertical order is irrelevant, it must be wired into something, and on its own it does nothing.
- 1:03:50→1:08:10 [TimeClip] · in-depth · scripted · Gotcha · 80% — Plain dragging a time clip moves only its enable-window, not the keyframes inside; Alt-drag instead and it time-remaps, stretching or compressing the inner animation's local time — powerful but very easy to get lost in.
- 1:08:40→1:10:00 · in-depth · discussion · Concept · 80% — Connection arrows point the way data flows, but evaluation runs the opposite direction: the output window pulls, requesting each upstream node for the value it needs.
- 1:09:50→1:11:10 [Time] [FloatToString] [Text] · explained · experiment · Example · 78% — Read the current playback time, stringify it, and route it into a text operator to build an on-screen time readout.
- 1:11:50→1:14:50 [TimeClip] · in-depth · answer · Concept · 80% — The remapped time-clip drives a child's local time from 0→1 over its window, and that same local time feeds the child's internal keyframes — the basis for reusable, retimeable chapter/title animations.
- 1:14:40→1:20:00 [SetTime] · in-depth · experiment · Concept · 80% — Overwrites the time flowing down the graph (absolute or relative), so a duplicated scene branch can run at a frozen or slowed-down time; note it re-runs the whole particle sim per branch.
- 1:21:30→1:23:30 [GridPoints] [CubeMesh] [RepeatMeshAtPoints] · explained · experiment · Example · 78% — Scatter cube instances over a single-row grid by drawing the mesh at grid points, adjusting the grid's Y count to one layer and the cube pivot to sit on the ground.
- 1:23:00→1:25:00 [QuadMesh] · explained · experiment · Gotcha · 68% — A quad faces the camera by default, so it must be rotated (negatively, oddly) to lie flat as a ground plane; subdividing it helps fog and shading interpolate smoothly.
- 1:23:50→1:24:50 [SetEnvironment] · in-depth · explained · Concept · 80% — Sets shared light and fog parameters consumed by every "draw" downstream; it isn't a shader, so a background not actually drawn (no backdrop mesh) won't pick up the fog.
- 1:25:30→1:28:40 [FractalNoise] [DrawMeshUnlit] [RepeatMeshAtPoints] · explained · experiment · Tip · 70% — A 90s-era cloud cheat: render fractal noise unlit to a texture, map transparent-white→white, and stack many offset planes via repeat-at-points to fake volumetric mist.
- 1:29:00→1:32:00 [SDFToColor] · in-depth · experiment · Example · 80% — Feed a plane SDF into a draw operator's color-field override and convert distance-to-ground into a gradient, so color climbs the geometry from the floor up.
- 1:32:00→1:33:00 [NoiseDisplaceSDF] · explained · experiment · Tip · 72% — Offsetting the color-driving plane field with animated noise warps where the gradient bands fall, and the displacement can be animated over time.
- 1:35:00→1:37:30 [DefineGradient] [BlendGradients] · explained · experiment · Tip · 70% — Break a gradient out of an operator to reuse its exact definition elsewhere; blending two gradients exists but crossfades alpha, which looks poor for hard color cycling.
- 1:41:50→1:43:10 [CombineFieldColor] [LinearGradient] · in-depth · experiment · Example · 68% — Stack two color fields and combine them as a proper multiply (not an alpha crossfade) to layer gradients for a scrolling color-cycle look.
- 1:43:00→1:45:00 [PushPullSDF] [RepeatField3] · in-depth · experiment · Gotcha · 65% — Cycling a color band by animating a push-pull offset only sweeps once; repeating the field to loop it must happen before the push-pull, or the repeat has no effect.
- 1:50:00→1:52:40 · explained · experiment · Gotcha · 70% — Faking volumetric ground fog by drawing many soft blob sprites needs z-buffer writing off and alpha not clipped, or the overlapping sprites cut into each other.
- 1:53:00→1:55:00 [TransformPoints] · explained · experiment · Comparison · 72% — Stretching point sprites via a point-space transform (scale/stretch) is far more efficient than scaling the drawn geometry, which wastes transparent pixels.
- 1:56:50→2:05:10 [ColorGrade] · in-depth · scripted · Parameters · 90% — Lift/gamma/gain is the core of pro color grading: lift moves shadows, gamma the midtones, gain the highlights, each neutral at gray; push gain down and midtones up to add contrast, and tint each band toward a hue for a teal-and-orange look. Includes a vignette band with its own radius.
- 1:57:00→1:58:40 [WaveForm] · explained · scripted · Concept · 80% — Shows the per-column color distribution of an image (plus a small vectorscope), so you can read which channels are clipping or where a tint sits before grading.
- 2:03:20→2:07:00 [ColorGradeDepth] · in-depth · scripted · Example · 82% — Feeds the depth buffer alongside the image so a gradient grades by distance — e.g. tinting the background cooler — for subtle, very effective atmospheric depth cueing.
- 2:08:20→2:11:00 [ToneMapping] · in-depth · scripted · Concept · 82% — Compresses over-bright values toward a center curve; the AGX mode desaturates blown-out colors so a clipping bright yellow rounds off instead of flat-lining the waveform into pure primary.
- 2:06:50→2:07:30 [Bloom] · passing · scripted · Tip · 60% — Flagged as a near-mandatory final glow pass, with a caution that what it blooms depends on whether grading clipped highlights first.
- 2:11:00→2:11:50 [Grain] · passing · discussion · Gotcha · 60% — Where grain sits in the chain — before or after tone mapping — changes the result dramatically, so its placement is a deliberate choice.
- 2:05:00→2:05:40 [AdjustColors] · passing · discussion · Comparison · 62% — Named as the hue/saturation/brightness tool many reach for, contrasted against lift/gamma/gain as the more powerful grading approach.
- 2:14:00→2:22:00 [MapPointAttributes] · in-depth · answer · Example · 82% — To invert a particle's F1 factor, set its input/output mode to F1 and draw a 0→1 / 1→0 curve in the built-in curve editor; ping-pong and phase modes let an F value repeat a pattern over time.
- 2:15:20→2:18:30 · explained · answer · Concept · 75% — F1 and F2 are per-particle factor channels (default 1, neutral); writing randomized values into them lets one factor drive emit velocity while another drives size, so left-vs-right particles differ.
- 2:19:30→2:22:00 [CustomPointShader] · in-depth · answer · Example · 78% — The shader alternative to factor remapping: write `pfx1 = 1 - pfx1` to invert a factor, and wrap it in `saturate()` to clamp 0–1 so tiny values don't go negative.
- 2:27:30→2:32:30 [SphereSDF] [VectorFieldForce] · in-depth · experiment · Example · 72% — Turn an SDF surface into a directional field and use it as a vector force so particles accelerate along the surface normal; invert the amount and the shape becomes an attractor you can drag through the cloud.

- 3:03:30→3:05:00 [DrawRibbons] [ExtrudeCurves] · discussion · experiment · Comparison · 65% — For shaded lines, extruding a curve into a mesh is the expensive route; draw-ribbons is the lighter option but is flagged as currently unreliable, with spin/pin parameters affecting the result.

- 3:22:00→3:30:00 [PlaybackFFT] [AudioReaction] · in-depth · discussion · Concept · 78% — FFT/audio-reaction read the global merged output buffer (the same master buffer written when exporting a video), which is why per-stem reactions aren't possible today without per-channel routing.
- 3:23:00→3:48:00 [ui:AudioInput] · in-depth · discussion · Concept · 70% — Why multi-stem audio is hard: audio runs on its own thread at the sound card's sample rate, fully decoupled from the frame loop, with re-seek jitter that must stay inside a ~40ms window or it stutters — far less forgiving than dropped video frames.
- 3:33:00→3:39:00 · in-depth · discussion · Concept · 65% — Proposed direction for stems: declare audio channels/tracks in the playback settings (stable across operator edits), let clips pick a target channel, and let reaction nodes pick which channel to analyze.
