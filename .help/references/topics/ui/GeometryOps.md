Operators that build and reshape geometry on the CPU, keeping real faces and shared points — the route to modelling that needs topology, such as bevelling edges or fracturing a solid into pieces.

Geometry starts at a source like [CubeGeometry], [LoadObjGeometry] or [TextToCurves] with [CurvesToGeometry], passes through ops such as [BevelGeometry], [VoronoiFracture], [SeparateLooseParts] and [TransformGeometry], and becomes drawable with [GeometryToMesh]. Faces are n-gons over shared points rather than loose triangles, so these ops know which corners belong together — that is what makes a clean bevel possible, and why the same shape as a plain mesh cannot be bevelled. Some of them take a scalar field to drive an effect per vertex or per part; those are evaluated on the CPU and are separate from the [ui:Field|shader-graph fields] used for raymarching. For a fractured solid, compile with [GeometryToMeshChunks] and draw it using [DrawMeshChunksAtPoints]: every piece becomes a point you can move, rotate or scatter without rebuilding any geometry.
